# L20 — Privacy, sovereignty and governance

**Stage:** original reference design. **Proposed accountable role:** Risk, privacy, jurisdiction and key owners.

**Inputs:** Entity obligations, classifications, operator/supplier boundaries.

**Outputs:** Applicable control decisions and bounded disclosure.

## Decisions, failure modes and acceptance evidence

| ID | Decision to make | Failure to avoid | Evidence required before acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|
| L20-C01 | Define data, administrator, key, support, supplier and recovery authority independently. | Local workload placement is presented as complete sovereignty. | Boundary register with enforcement, contractual and observed evidence separated. |
| L20-C02 | Establish entity/jurisdiction/purpose applicability and source versions. | Framework mappings are mistaken for legal compliance or national accreditation. | Requirements register reviewed by authorized domain advisers. |
| L20-C03 | Minimize collection, AI context and exports according to approved purpose. | Security monitoring becomes uncontrolled sensitive-data accumulation. | Field-level rationale, retention and denied disclosure cases. |
| L20-C04 | Control cross-border replication, support, analytics and recovery paths. | A local primary database masks foreign backups or remote administrator access. | Workload/evidence/key/recovery placement map and independent assurance gaps. |
| L20-C05 | Define supplier assurance, exit, access and notification responsibilities. | Critical provider changes or failure leave no local owner or alternative. | Supplier review, dependency/exit plan and joint failure exercise. |
| L20-C06 | Give risks, exceptions and policy changes accountable owners and expiry. | Permanent exceptions silently weaken the architecture. | Accepted residual-risk record, expiration and re-evaluation trigger. |

## Review this layer

Identify the actual service, tenant, jurisdiction and implementation scope. Replace proposed owner roles with accepted responsibilities. Exercise intended, denied, degraded and recovery cases. Record what was observed and what remains unknown; a complete design record does not establish enforcement.
